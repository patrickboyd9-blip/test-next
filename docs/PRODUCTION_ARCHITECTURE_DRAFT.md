# Modern Mail Production Architecture (Draft)

> **Status:** Draft
>
> This document captures the evolving architecture of Modern Mail's production subsystem. It serves as the design workspace before these decisions are incorporated into `PROJECT_BLUEPRINT.md`.
>
> The goal of this document is to define a vendor-neutral production architecture capable of supporting multiple fulfillment providers (Click2Mail, Lob, future providers) without exposing provider-specific concepts to the rest of the application.

---

# Design Philosophy

Modern Mail owns the business domain.

Fulfillment providers are implementation details.

The platform should speak the language of direct mail, not the language of individual vendors. Every architectural decision in this document should reinforce that separation of concerns.

The production subsystem is responsible for transforming an approved marketing concept into a manufacturable mail piece while remaining independent of any specific fulfillment provider.

---

# Architectural Principles

## Principle 1

**Modern Mail owns the business domain. Fulfillment providers are implementation details.**

The core application should never depend directly on Click2Mail or any other vendor. All provider-specific behavior must be isolated behind adapter interfaces.

---

## Principle 2

**AI generates creative within deterministic constraints defined by the platform.**

Business rules, production constraints, campaign strategy, and validation belong to deterministic software.

AI is responsible for creative expression—not business logic.

---

## Principle 3

**The Strategy Engine is the sole owner of `MailPieceSpec`.**

The Strategy Engine determines what should be produced.

Creative AI consumes the specification but never defines or modifies it.

---

## Principle 4

**Strategy recommendations are evidence-based, explainable, and continuously improve through accumulated platform knowledge—not by increasing AI autonomy.**

The Strategy Engine evolves through measurable campaign outcomes, industry playbooks, platform analytics, and customer history.

Recommendations should always be explainable and supported by evidence.

# Core Domain Objects

## Campaign

Owns the overall business workflow.

---

## Strategy

Produces campaign recommendations and generates the canonical `MailPieceSpec`.

---

## MailPieceSpec

Defines the physical and strategic blueprint for the mail piece before any creative generation occurs.

Examples include:

- mail format
- dimensions
- layout template
- production constraints
- required assets
- reserved address area
- branding requirements

This object is deterministic and vendor-neutral.

---

## MailPiece

Represents the completed creative design after user approval.

This is the canonical marketing artifact owned by Modern Mail.

It is independent of any fulfillment provider.

---

## Renderer

Transforms a `MailPiece` into one or more production-ready artifacts while enforcing print requirements such as bleed, trim, safe zones, image resolution, and typography.

---

## ProductionDocument

Represents a manufacturable artifact generated by the renderer.

Examples may include:

- print-ready PDF
- production proof
- preview images

This object remains vendor-neutral.

---

## Production Provider

Coordinates the fulfillment workflow while remaining independent of any specific vendor.

---

## Vendor Adapter

Translates Modern Mail production objects into vendor-specific API requests.

Examples:

- Click2Mail
- Lob
- Future providers

The rest of Modern Mail should never depend directly on vendor-specific terminology or APIs.

# Architectural Decision Records

## ADR-001: Introduce `MailPieceSpec` as a Distinct Domain Object

### Status

Accepted (Draft)

### Decision

`MailPieceSpec` is a distinct domain object within Modern Mail's production architecture.

It is the canonical, vendor-neutral specification of the physical mail product to be created.

### Rationale

`CampaignBrief`, `CampaignStrategy`, `CreativeSpec`, and `MailPieceSpec` each represent different business concepts.

- **CampaignBrief** defines what the customer wants.
- **CampaignStrategy** defines what Modern Mail recommends.
- **MailPieceSpec** defines the physical mail product to be created.
- **CreativeSpec** defines the creative expression of that physical product.

`MailPieceSpec` exists because the physical characteristics of a mail piece are neither marketing strategy nor creative execution.

Examples include:

- mail format (postcard, letter, self-mailer)
- dimensions
- orientation
- production template
- reserved address area
- required production elements
- print constraints

These characteristics define the canvas on which creative is produced.

### Domain Test

A domain object should exist only if it represents a unique business concept with its own responsibilities and future evolution.

The deciding question was:

> Could multiple `MailPieceSpec`s legitimately exist for the same `CampaignStrategy`?

The answer is yes.

A single campaign strategy may intentionally produce multiple physical implementations, such as:

- 5×8 postcard
- 6×11 postcard
- letter package

while preserving the same audience, offer, messaging, and marketing objectives.

Because multiple `MailPieceSpec`s may exist for a single `CampaignStrategy`, they are distinct business concepts rather than different views of the same object.

### Consequences

The Strategy Engine owns the creation of `MailPieceSpec`.

Creative systems consume `MailPieceSpec` but do not modify it.

Production providers consume artifacts derived from `MailPiece`, never `CampaignStrategy`.

## ADR-002: `MailPieceSpec` References an Immutable Catalog Version

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

`MailPieceSpec` will reference a specific, immutable version of a canonical Modern Mail Catalog definition rather than copying the catalog's technical specifications into the campaign.

The relationship is:

```
Mail Piece Catalog
        │
        ▼
Mail Piece Catalog Version
        │
        ▼
MailPieceSpec
        │
        ▼
Campaign
```

The catalog version remains the authoritative definition of the physical mail piece.

`MailPieceSpec` records:

- selected catalog entry/version
- campaign-specific selections
- permitted campaign-specific options/overrides
- recommendation/selection context where appropriate
- approval/decision information

`MailPieceSpec` must **not** duplicate the catalog's canonical physical, creative, or production constraints.

Campaign-specific overrides are only permitted where the catalog explicitly allows that characteristic to vary. Hard physical constraints cannot simply be overridden by a campaign.

Catalog versions are immutable. If a supported mail-piece definition changes, Modern Mail creates a new catalog version rather than modifying the existing version.

Example:

- Campaign A → `MailPieceSpec` → 5×8 Postcard v1
- Campaign B → `MailPieceSpec` → 5×8 Postcard v2

Changing v2 must never alter Campaign A's interpretation of v1.

### Rationale

The catalog answers what Modern Mail can produce. `MailPieceSpec` answers what this campaign selected.

Copying catalog constraints onto the campaign would create a second, drifting source of truth. A versioned reference keeps physical, creative, and production constraints canonical on the catalog while still allowing a campaign to record selection, permitted options, and decision context.

Immutability preserves historical campaigns. A later catalog revision is a new version, not an in-place edit of a version already referenced.

### Consequences

Campaigns depend on a catalog entry **and** a catalog version, not on an inlined snapshot of physical specifications.

Overrides that the catalog does not declare as variable are invalid, even if a campaign requests them.

Beta scope: use an immutable catalog-version reference. Do not build a separate resolved production snapshot yet. A future production/audit layer may persist resolved constraints when a production artifact is created or submitted, but that is not the primary source of truth for `MailPieceSpec`.

This decision does not implement `MailPieceSpec`, change the catalog module, or change `Campaign`. Those remain later implementation steps.
