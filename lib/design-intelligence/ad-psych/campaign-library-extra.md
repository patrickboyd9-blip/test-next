# Campaign Library: Extra Verticals (from C-3PO's prospect list)

> **BASELINE (Patrick's hard rule):** every mailer must offer a sell-the-outcome option. **Emotion-neutral:** fear and joy are both valid. The only limits are law and compliance.

Prepared Oct 20, 2026 for Modern Mail. Principle names refer to `postcard-principles.md` (P#) and `emotion-needs-madmen-report.md`.
**Evidence rules:** Every campaign listed is real and publicly documented. **"Results"** are given only with a source. A claim reported only by the brand or its agency (including award-entry case studies) is marked **[unverified – company/agency claim]**. Where no results were published, it says **"No published results."** Niches with fewer than 5 documented campaigns are listed short and **not padded**. Merged niches are noted.
**URL status:** each source URL was checked for a live HTTP response on Oct 20, 2026 (see the Verification log at the end). A live page confirms that the source exists. It does not confirm every number on it.

Format per campaign: **Brand – Campaign (year, medium).** Idea · Psychology · Results · Lesson for postcards · Compliance.

## 1. HVAC
1. **Carrier – "Turn to the Experts" (1990s–present, TV/print/dealer co-op).** Idea: Willis Carrier invented modern AC, so Carrier owns "the experts." Psychology: authority (P: Authority), owning a true claim (Mad Men "own a true fact"). Results: No published results. **Confirmed:** "Turn to the Experts" is Carrier's brand tagline (dealer and trade usage). Source: https://www.carrier.com/residential/en/us/about-carrier/ · Lesson: lead with the one true credential the competitor can't claim. · Compliance: "expert/#1" claims need substantiation (FTC Act §5).
2. **Trane – "It's Hard to Stop a Trane" (1992–present, TV).** Idea: torture-test spots showing durability. Psychology: demonstration, loss aversion (breakdowns), proof. Results: No published results. **Confirmed:** Trane introduced the tagline in 1992. Source: https://www.trane.com/residential/en/resources/blog/press-releases/legacy-innovation-trane-celebrates-century-service/ · Lesson: one vivid durability image beats a list of features. · Compliance: dramatized tests must be truthful (FTC demonstration doctrine).
3. **ENERGY STAR (EPA) – heating/cooling efficiency education (1992–present, multichannel).** Idea: a government-backed label signals savings. Psychology: authority, reducing the risk of choosing, anchoring on dollar savings. Results: EPA reports more than 90% household recognition of the label (agency data). Source: https://www.energystar.gov/about · Lesson: show any real certification badge clearly; it lowers perceived risk. · Compliance: use the mark only per EPA brand rules; never imply you *are* the government.
Note: the Lennox entry was cut (unconfirmed). **Count: 3.**
**Patterns:** (a) Authority and longevity of the brand. (b) Comfort and family outcomes vs. fear of breakdown in extreme weather; both work, but give one easy action. (c) Rebate and efficiency numbers anchor value; cite the real program.

## 2. Plumbing
1. **Roto-Rooter – "Away go troubles down the drain" jingle (1954–present, radio/TV).** Idea: a sticky jingle promising relief. Psychology: mere exposure, processing fluency, relief (sell the outcome). Results: No sales data published. **Confirmed:** written in 1954 by Captain Stubby and the Buccaneers (WLS Chicago), in continuous use since; the company calls it the oldest continuously used jingle [unverified – company claim]. Sources: https://www.rotorooter.com/blog/plumbing/roto-rooter-jingle-turns-67/ · https://www.pmmag.com/articles/86409-roto-rooter-jingle-turns-50 · Lesson: a short rhythmic tagline aids recall at the moment of crisis. · Compliance: none.
2. **Benjamin Franklin Plumbing – "The Punctual Plumber" on-time guarantee (2000s–present, all media).** Idea: a punctuality guarantee that names the customer's top fear. Psychology: risk reversal, loss aversion, a concrete promise. Results: No published results. **Confirmed terms:** $5 for every minute late, up to $300 (terms vary by franchise). Source: https://www.benjaminfranklinplumbing.com/about-us/our-guarantees/ · Lesson: a specific guarantee answers the #1 objection. · Compliance: the guarantee terms must be disclosed and honored (FTC guarantee guidance).
3. **Fix a Leak Week (EPA WaterSense, 2009–present, PR/social).** Idea: an annual "holiday" that dramatizes hidden leaks (the EPA says household leaks can waste nearly 10,000 gallons a year). Psychology: fear of hidden loss, a social event, timeliness. Results: EPA figure as cited. Source: https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week · Lesson: piggyback a date-based hook ("It's Fix a Leak Week"). · Compliance: cite EPA numbers accurately; no government look-alike design.
Note: **Count: 3.** (few plumbing campaigns are documented with results).
**Patterns:** (a) Emergency relief and the promise of speed. (b) Guarantees aimed at the fear of a no-show or a surprise bill. (c) Memorable names and jingles that stick before the emergency.

## 4. Pest control (incl. natural termite) + consumer pest-repellent products (merged)
1. **Orkin – "The Orkin Man" (1954–present, TV).** Idea: a heroic exterminator vs. cartoon bugs. Psychology: disgust (Plutchik), a hero archetype, humor. Results: No published sales results. **Confirmed:** first TV ad in 1954 (Otto the Orkin Man, a singing spray can); updated to "Orkin Pro" in 2022. Sources: https://www.orkinglobal.com/why-orkin/ · https://www.mypmp.net/5-questions-with-cam-glover/ · Lesson: disgust gets attention; pair it with an instant, confident fix. · Compliance: pesticide claims must follow the label (FIFRA; EPA prohibits "safe/non-toxic" claims on registered pesticides).
2. **Raid – "Kills bugs dead" (long-running tagline; start year not confirmed).** Idea: a blunt promise of the outcome. Psychology: processing fluency, certainty. Results: No published results; the tagline's longevity is documented. Source: period slogan lists (e.g., https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/en/l/i/s/List_of_advertising_slogans.html) · Lesson: a five-word outcome headline. · Compliance: label-consistent claims only (FIFRA).
3. **OFF! – "Mosquitoes and Zika" public-health education (2016, digital/PR).** Idea: tied the product to a real health threat. Psychology: fear plus efficacy (EPPM: the threat plus an easy action). Results: No published sales results. **Confirmed:** SC Johnson Zika education hub, product donations to CDC Zika Prevention Kits, and OFF! as the first insect-repellent partner of Rio 2016. Sources: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sc-johnson-continues-educating-consumers-about-mosquito-protection-300281869.html · https://fortune.com/2016/07/05/off-insect-rio-olympics/ · Lesson: when fear is real news, give the one-step action. · Compliance: health claims must match the EPA label; avoid overclaiming disease prevention.
4. **Mortein – "Louie the Fly" (Australia, 1957–present, TV; jingle from 1962).** Idea: a cartoon villain fly who fears the can. Psychology: disgust personified, a villain archetype, a jingle (mere exposure). Results: ran for about 55 years; ranked 5th in the Advertising Federation of Australia's 50 best ads (2006). Sources: https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/item/louie-fly-mortein-jingle-1962 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louie_the_Fly · Lesson: make the problem a character the reader wants defeated. · Compliance: label-consistent claims (Australia: APVMA).
**Natural termite:** no documented campaign with results was found. **Count: 4.**
**Patterns:** (a) Disgust and fear are the native emotions; pair them with an instant fix. (b) The invisible threat of damage that isn't covered. (c) Strict pesticide claim rules: no "safe," "non-toxic," or "harmless" on registered products. Application must follow the label: in 2017 Terminix pleaded guilty to FIFRA misdemeanors over illegal methyl bromide fumigation in the USVI, with $10M in total payments (https://www.epa.gov/archive/epa/newsreleases/terminix-companies-sentenced-applying-restricted-use-pesticide-residences-us-virgin.html). The Terminix campaign entry was cut because its title was unconfirmed.
