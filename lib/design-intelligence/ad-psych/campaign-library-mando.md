# Psychologically Effective Ad Campaigns — Mando's Extra Verticals
Modern Mail research library · compiled 2026-10-20 (PT) · emotion-neutral (fear, joy, pride, guilt, etc. are all usable). Hard limits are law and compliance only. Baseline rule: every mailer gives the client the option to **sell the outcome**.

**How to read this file**
- Every campaign is real. Each has a source URL. A result that comes only from the brand, its agency or an award entry is marked **(unverified)**. Award-entry numbers are self-reported.
- A vertical lists fewer than 5 campaigns, or none, when we couldn't find famous, documented campaigns. We left those gaps open and didn't fill them. Section Z lists the verticals with 0 campaigns, along with their real mailer evidence.
- "Mailer evidence" means MMR IDs from Mando's library at /home/box/modern-mail-research/examples/. Read-only. We didn't edit it.
- Wikipedia links are entry points. The primary citations are listed there. DOI links are the peer-reviewed sources.

## A. Nonprofit, civic & public-benefit (the strongest *mail* evidence of any group)

### Political turnout
1. **"Social Pressure" turnout mailers (Gerber, Green & Larimer), 2006 Michigan primary. Medium: direct mail.**
   - Core idea: one of several mailers. The strongest one listed the voting records of the household and its neighbors, and said an updated list would be mailed after the election.
   - Psychology: social pressure and injunctive norms; accountability and being observed; reputational concern.
   - Results: the "Neighbors" mailer raised turnout by about 8.1 percentage points (≈37.8% vs. 29.7% control). The milder "Self" and "Hawthorne" versions had smaller effects. Source: American Political Science Review 2008, https://doi.org/10.1017/S000305540808009X
   - Lesson for postcards: telling people that their peers can see their behavior is one of the strongest *proven-by-mail* levers there is. Gentler versions, like "your neighbors already did X," transfer to commercial mail.
   - Compliance: publishing someone's vote history is public record in many states but often triggers backlash. Political mail needs a "paid for by" disclaimer under FEC and state law. Using neighbors' data in commercial mail raises privacy and FTC issues, so use aggregate norms only.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-381, MMR-561.

### Organ donor registry
1. **Default effect, opt-in vs. opt-out countries (Johnson & Goldstein), 2003. Medium: policy/form design.**
   - Psychology: defaults, inertia, implied endorsement.
   - Results: consent rates were dramatically higher in opt-out countries than in opt-in ones. Source: Science 302:1338, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091721
   - Lesson: pre-select the desired choice ("☑ Yes, reserve my spot"). Making the reply effortless beats persuasion copy.
   - Compliance: a commercial "default" must not create a charge without express consent (ROSCA, negative-option rules).
2. **UK organ-donor registration message trial (Behavioural Insights Team with NHS Blood & Transplant), 2013. Medium: a webpage prompt seen by about 1M people.**
   - Psychology: reciprocity framing ("If you needed a transplant would you have one? If so please help others") vs. social-proof and image variants.
   - Results: the reciprocity line produced the most registrations. BIT estimated roughly 100k extra registrations a year **(BIT-reported; confirm against the BIT trial paper before quoting numbers)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioural_Insights_Team
   - Lesson: one question that makes the reader imagine being the beneficiary beats statistics. It also showed that *adding a stock photo of people lowered response*.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-345 is credit-union "organic growth", not organ donation, so there is no true organ-donor mailer in the library.

### Public-benefit outreach (tax, SNAP, Medicaid)
1. **HMRC tax-payment letters (Behavioural Insights Team), 2011–12. Medium: letters.**
   - Psychology: descriptive social norms ("9 out of 10 people in your area pay on time"), local reference group.
   - Results: norm lines raised on-time payment vs. the standard letter. The local-norm version performed best. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioural_Insights_Team (primary: Hallsworth et al., J. Public Economics 2017) **(confirm exact lift before quoting)**.
   - Lesson: "most people like you already did this" works in a mail format, and a local reference group is stronger than a national one.
   - Compliance: never imply government affiliation in commercial mail (deceptive mailings statute 39 U.S.C. §3001(h)–(i), state laws).
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-534 (SNAP), MMR-549 (homestead exemption), MMR-414.

### Charities & legacy giving
1. **ALS Ice Bucket Challenge (ALS Association), 2014. Medium: social video/UGC.**
   - Psychology: social proof, public commitment, tagging (reciprocity and social obligation), novelty.
   - Results: about $115M raised by ALSA in roughly 8 weeks, widely reported. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Bucket_Challenge
   - Lesson: give people a visible way to show they took part (a "pass it on to a neighbor" tear-off).
   - Compliance: charitable solicitation registration in each state; the use of funds must match the appeal.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-471, MMR-634.

### Food banks / hospital foundations
- We found no famous campaign with verified results specific to these. Use the charity patterns above. Mailer evidence: MMR-510 (food-bank sustainers), MMR-509, MMR-537 (hospital foundation).

### Girl Scouts
1. **Girl Scout Cookie program (Girl Scouts of the USA), 1917 to present. Medium: in-person and booth sales, now digital.**
   - Psychology: scarcity and seasonality (once-a-year window), liking (the seller is a child or neighbor), reciprocity, cause halo.
   - Results: a long-running national program that runs on its own scarcity calendar. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Scout_cookie
   - Lesson: a predictable annual "season" with a hard end trains customers to buy in advance ("Our spring tune-up window closes May 31").
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-498.

**Patterns (nonprofit/civic):** (1) In mail, social norms and the sense of being observed are the most rigorously proven levers. (2) Defaults and low-friction reply mechanisms beat clever copy. (3) Reciprocity framed as "if it were you" beats statistics and stock photos.

## B. Utilities, energy & water

### Energy switching / energy efficiency
1. **Opower Home Energy Reports, 2008 onward. Medium: mailed letters.**
   - Core idea: compares your usage to similar neighbors, with a 🙂 rating for efficient homes.
   - Psychology: descriptive and injunctive norms, competition, feedback.
   - Results: about a 2.0% average reduction in consumption across randomized programs. Source: Allcott, J. Public Economics 2011, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2011.03.003 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opower
   - Lesson: a simple "you vs. efficient neighbors" chart on a postcard is a proven *mailed* behavior changer.
   - Compliance: savings claims need substantiation (FTC). Energy switching has state PUC marketing rules, including disclosure of variable-rate terms.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-597 (Ofgem switching), MMR-364.

### Water conservation
1. **Normative feedback on door hangers (Schultz et al.), 2007. Medium: door hangers.**
   - Psychology: descriptive norms with the "boomerang" effect. Low users told the average drifted *up*, and adding an injunctive 🙂 prevented that.
   - Results: published field experiment. Source: Psychological Science 18(5), https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01917.x
   - Lesson: when you cite "average," add an approval cue for people already doing well, or you can backfire on them.

### Wireless
1. **T-Mobile "Un-carrier," 2013 onward. Medium: TV, events, PR.**
   - Psychology: enemy framing against category pain (contracts, overages), reactance, contrast, paying off switching fees to cut loss aversion.
   - Results: T-Mobile became one of the industry's leading net-adders in the following years **(company-reported, unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un-carrier
   - Lesson: name the category's hated practice and abolish it. "No contracts. We pay your cancellation fee."
   - Compliance: "we pay your ETF" offers must disclose caps and conditions (FTC, FCC truth-in-billing).
2. **Verizon "Can you hear me now?", 2002–2011. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: repetition and fluency, a demonstration of reliability, a catchphrase.
   - Results: a long-running iconic campaign. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_you_hear_me_now%3F
   - Lesson: dramatize one proof point (reliability) repeatedly, not ten features.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-395.

### Satellite radio
- No campaign with verified results. Mailer evidence: MMR-631 (SiriusXM Canada).

**Patterns:** (1) Neighbor comparison is proven by mail. (2) An enemy (the category's pain) plus removing the switching cost works for switching offers. (3) Repeat one proof point until it becomes the brand.

## C. Restaurants: QSR, pizza, pubs, buffets, birthday and loyalty programs

1. **Burger King "Whopper Detour," 2018. Medium: app, geofencing, PR.**
   - Core idea: a 1¢ Whopper unlocked only within 600 ft of a McDonald's.
   - Psychology: rivalry and humor, gamification, a price anchor of 1¢, curiosity, earned media.
   - Results: 1.5M+ app downloads in 9 days and 500k+ redemptions. One Club entry says 27:1 ROI, Adweek quotes the chain at 37:1, and the two numbers conflict **(company/award-reported, unverified)**. Won Grand Prix in Direct, Mobile and Titanium at Cannes 2019. Sources: https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-award/33272/the-whopper-detour/ · https://www.adweek.com/creativity/whopper-detour-burger-kings-masterpiece-of-trolling-wins-direct-grand-prix-at-cannes/
   - Lesson: an offer with a fun "condition" (redeem at a place, a date, a code) turns a coupon into a game.
   - Compliance: comparative ads must be truthful (Lanham Act). Give real access to the 1¢ price as advertised.
2. **Domino's "Pizza Turnaround," Dec 2009. Medium: TV and documentary-style web.**
   - Psychology: candor (admitted the pizza was bad), pratfall effect, credibility from self-criticism.
   - Results: Q1 2010 U.S. same-store sales up 14.3%, reported in company earnings (public filing). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino%27s_Pizza
   - Lesson: "We heard you. Here's what we fixed." is a powerful reactivation card for lapsed customers.
3. **Wendy's "Where's the Beef?", 1984. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: humor, comparative contrast, a catchphrase that turned into a meme.
   - Results: widely reported revenue increase **(unverified magnitude)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_the_beef%3F
   - Lesson: a short challenge line that implies the competitor shortchanges you.
4. **Chick-fil-A "Eat Mor Chikin" Cows, 1995 onward. Medium: billboard, then everything.**
   - Psychology: humor, an absurd mascot (distinctive asset), self-interest framed from the cow's point of view.
   - Results: a long-running brand asset **(sales attribution unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eat_Mor_Chikin
   - Lesson: one ownable character or device, repeated across every drop, builds recognition in the mailbox.
5. **Denny's free Grand Slam (Super Bowl 2009). Medium: TV, in-store event.**
   - Psychology: "free" as a zero-price effect, a one-day event (urgency), reciprocity.
   - Results: about 2M free breakfasts served **(company-reported)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny%27s
   - Lesson: birthday and anniversary "free entrée" cards rest on the same zero-price-plus-date logic.
   - Compliance: "free" must be truly free, with conditions disclosed nearby (FTC Guide on "Free," 16 CFR 251).
- Mailer evidence: pizza MMR-170, MMR-570; pub MMR-272, MMR-492; buffet MMR-646; birthday program MMR-404; QSR MMR-186.

**Patterns:** (1) Gamified or conditional offers beat plain discounts. (2) Candor and humor create attention and credibility. (3) Birthday and date-based "free" offers use the zero-price effect plus a deadline.

## D. Retail

### Department stores
1. **JCPenney "Fair and Square" (ending coupons), 2012. Medium: TV, print, in-store. *Negative case.***
   - Psychology: removing the anchor and the "deal" thrill. It ignored transaction utility, the joy of the deal.
   - Results: annual sales fell about 25%, and the CEO was ousted in 2013. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._Penney
   - Lesson: coupon shoppers buy the *feeling of winning*. Keep a reference price, show the "you save" amount, and add a deadline.
   - Compliance: reference prices must be bona fide former prices (FTC 16 CFR 233).
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-290, MMR-446.

### Furniture
1. **IKEA "Lamp" (Spike Jonze / Crispin Porter + Bogusky), 2002. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: anthropomorphism and pity, then a twist ("you're crazy, it has no feelings"). Humor licenses the switch to new things.
   - Results: Grand Prix at Cannes 2003. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamp_(advertisement)
   - Lesson: give permission to replace old stuff, so the guilt of discarding it doesn't block the purchase.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-478, MMR-416.

### Sporting goods / apparel / footwear
1. **REI #OptOutside, 2015. Medium: PR, social.**
   - Core idea: closed stores on Black Friday and paid staff to go outside.
   - Psychology: values signaling, identity, counter-conformity (reactance against Black Friday).
   - Results: REI reported about 1.4M people pledging participation **(company-reported, unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_Equipment,_Inc.
   - Lesson: a stance that matches the customer's identity can beat a discount.
2. **Nike "Just Do It," 1988. Medium: TV, print.**
   - Psychology: identity and self-efficacy, aspiration (the Maslow esteem and self-actualization levels).
   - Results: the often-quoted share growth is **unverified (folklore-level)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Do_It
   - Lesson: sell who the customer becomes, the outcome, not the product.
3. **Patagonia "Don't Buy This Jacket" (NYT full page, Black Friday 2011). Medium: print.**
   - Psychology: paradoxical intention and reactance, costly signaling, pratfall and honesty.
   - Results: the reported sales growth afterward is **unverified/causality disputed**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonia,_Inc.
   - Lesson: a counterintuitive headline stops the scroll, or the mailbox sort.
4. **TOMS "One for One," 2006–2019. Medium: word of mouth, retail.**
   - Psychology: warm glow, moral licensing, identity.
   - Results: grew fast. The model was criticized and dropped in 2019. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOMS_Shoes
   - Lesson: tie the purchase to a concrete good, and make the claim exact and true.
   - Compliance: cause-marketing claims must be accurate. Some states regulate commercial co-ventures.
- Mailer evidence: sporting goods MMR-626; apparel MMR-237; lingerie MMR-625; footwear MMR-317.

### Wine & craft beer
1. **Miller Lite "Tastes Great! Less Filling!", 1974 onward. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: removing the either/or trade-off, playful debate, celebrity (ex-athletes) countering the "diet" stigma.
   - Results: credited with building the U.S. light-beer category. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Lite
2. **Dos Equis "The Most Interesting Man in the World," 2006–2018. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: aspiration, humor, a distinctive character.
   - Results: reported strong U.S. growth **(unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Most_Interesting_Man_in_the_World
   - Compliance: alcohol ads follow TTB rules and state laws, and industry codes require 21+ audiences (at least ~71.6% adult audience). Don't mail to minors.
- Mailer evidence: MMR-222, MMR-294, MMR-608.

### Warehouse clubs
- No campaign with verified results. Costco famously does little advertising. Mailer evidence: MMR-535 (Costco Canada).

**Patterns:** (1) Shoppers buy the *feeling of a deal* (JCPenney), so keep reference prices honest and visible. (2) Identity and values sell apparel and outdoor gear. (3) Humor or paradox wins attention. Alcohol needs strict audience compliance.

## E. Financial & legal

### Banks
1. **Bank of America "Keep the Change" (designed with IDEO), 2005. Medium: product plus advertising.**
   - Psychology: mental accounting, painless saving through habit, rounding up.
   - Results: about 2.5M enrollments in year one is commonly cited **(company-reported, unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keep_the_Change
   - Lesson: a feature that makes the good behavior automatic sells itself. Show the outcome ("$X saved without trying").
   - Compliance: Truth in Savings (Reg DD). Advertised APY disclosures are required.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-329.

### Credit unions
1. **Bank Transfer Day, Nov 2011. Medium: grassroots social (Facebook), backed by CUs.**
   - Psychology: reactance against bank fees, an us-vs-them in-group, a date-certain collective action.
   - Results: CUNA reported about 650k new CU members in the month **(trade association-reported)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Transfer_Day
   - Lesson: "switch day" events, with pre-filled switch kits, use anger and belonging together.
   - Compliance: NCUA advertising rules, including the "Federally insured by NCUA" statement.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-033, MMR-263, MMR-344.

### Tax prep & tax resolution
1. **Intuit TurboTax "Free, free, free…" ads. *Negative/compliance case.* Medium: TV, digital.**
   - Psychology: zero-price effect, repetition.
   - Results: in Jan 2024 the FTC found the ads deceptive, saying about 2/3 of filers didn't qualify. The 5th Circuit vacated that order on constitutional grounds in Mar 2026, and the case was remanded. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-issues-opinion-finding-turbotax-maker-intuit-inc-engaged-deceptive-practices · https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/24-60040/24-60040-2026-03-20.html
   - Lesson: "free" pulls hard, so put the qualifier right next to the claim.
   - Compliance: tax resolution also faces FTC and state AG scrutiny over "pennies on the dollar" claims. Never imply an IRS affiliation.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-048 (prep), MMR-132 (resolution).

### Criminal defense & DUI
1. **"Buzzed Driving Is Drunk Driving" (NHTSA / Ad Council), 2005 onward. Medium: PSA.**
   - Psychology: reframing ("buzzed" counts as drunk), closing the loophole people use for self-justification, fear of consequences.
   - Results: an iconic long-running PSA **(impact attribution not verified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzzed_driving
   - Lesson for defense-attorney mail: the same consequence-awareness works in reverse ("A DUI can cost your license. Here's what to do in the next 10 days").
   - Compliance: state bar rules on targeted solicitation of arrestees. Many states require "ADVERTISING MATERIAL" labels, waiting periods (e.g. Florida's 30-day rule for some matters) and filing with the bar.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-350, MMR-368.

### Family law, estate planning, bail bonds
- No campaign with verified results. Mailer evidence: family/estate MMR-320, MMR-599; bail MMR-591. The bar and state bail-agent rules on solicitation apply.

**Patterns:** (1) Automate the good behavior and show the outcome. (2) In-group anger at fees drives switching. (3) Legal and financial "free" or trigger mail is heavily regulated, so keep qualifiers near the claim and follow bar labeling rules.

## F. Auto

### Dealers
1. **Hyundai Assurance (job-loss return program), Jan 2009. Medium: TV (Super Bowl).**
   - Psychology: loss aversion and risk reversal during the recession, empathy.
   - Results: Hyundai's U.S. sales grew in early 2009 while the industry collapsed, widely reported **(magnitude attributed by Hyundai, unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_America
   - Lesson: a guarantee that removes the buyer's *biggest fear right now* outsells a discount.
   - Compliance: guarantee terms must be clear and conspicuous (FTC). State auto-ad rules apply (advertised price, VIN and stock disclosures).
2. **Saturn "A different kind of company," no-haggle pricing, 1990. Medium: TV, retail model.**
   - Psychology: fairness, reducing anxiety and buyer's remorse, community (owner "homecoming" events).
   - Results: high customer-satisfaction rankings. The brand was discontinued in 2010. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_Corporation
   - Lesson: removing the dreaded part of the process, haggling, can be the headline.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-030, MMR-220, MMR-360.

### Tire & auto repair
1. **Michelin "Because so much is riding on your tires" (baby in a tire, DDB), 1985. Medium: TV, print.**
   - Psychology: fear and protection appeals, the parental protective instinct, Maslow's safety level.
   - Results: an iconic long-running line **(sales effect unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Man
   - Lesson: show *who* the service protects, not the tread.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-131, MMR-236, MMR-412.

### Collision, RV, towing, car sharing
- No campaign with verified results. Mailer evidence: collision MMR-546; RV MMR-556 (RV campground); towing MMR-134; car sharing MMR-621.

**Patterns:** (1) Risk reversal on the buyer's top fear. (2) Remove the most-hated step. (3) Protect-your-family imagery for safety services.

## G. Health (excludes dental and senior living, which are covered elsewhere)

### Addiction treatment / recovery
1. **CDC "Tips From Former Smokers," 2012 onward. Medium: TV, digital.**
   - Psychology: graphic fear with real people's stories, paired with a clear action (1-800-QUIT-NOW), which is the fear-plus-efficacy finding.
   - Results: an estimated 1.6M additional quit attempts and about 100k sustained quits from the 2012 campaign. Source: McAfee et al., Lancet 2013, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)61686-4
   - Lesson: strong fear works when the card gives one easy next step.
2. **"truth" anti-smoking campaign (American Legacy Foundation), 2000. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: reactance redirected at manipulative tobacco companies, teen autonomy and rebellion.
   - Results: associated with about 22% of the decline in youth smoking from 1999 to 2002. Source: Farrelly et al., AJPH 2005, https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2004.049692 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_Initiative
   - Lesson: give the audience an enemy that isn't themselves.
3. **"This Is Your Brain on Drugs" (Partnership for a Drug-Free America), 1987. Medium: TV PSA. *Cautionary.***
   - Psychology: a simple metaphor and shock.
   - Results: famous and parodied, and its evidence of behavior change is weak. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Your_Brain_on_Drugs
   - Lesson: being memorable is not the same as being effective, so measure response.
   - Compliance: treatment marketing faces SAMHSA, state patient-brokering laws (e.g. Florida), and Google and LegitScript certification for ads. HIPAA applies to any patient data.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-163, MMR-503.

### Cardiology
1. **AHA "Go Red for Women," 2004 onward. Medium: multichannel.**
   - Psychology: identity and in-group, reframing heart disease as a *women's* risk, an ownable color.
   - Results: AHA surveys showed women's awareness that heart disease is their leading killer rose from about 30% (1997) to 56% (2012). Source: Mosca et al., Circulation 2013 (cited at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Red_for_Women)
   - Lesson: correct a misconception ("This isn't just a man's disease") to open the door to screening.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-550.

### CPR training
1. **British Heart Foundation Hands-only CPR "Vinnie Jones / Stayin' Alive," 2012. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: humor reduces fear, a song gives the beat (fluency), self-efficacy ("anyone can do it").
   - Results: BHF has credited the ad with lives saved **(charity-reported, unverified)**. Source: https://www.bhf.org.uk/how-you-can-help/how-to-save-a-life/how-to-do-cpr/hands-only-cpr
   - Lesson: show that the skill is easy, which removes the "I can't" barrier.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-501.

### Optical
1. **Warby Parker Home Try-On, 2010. Medium: DTC mail and product.**
   - Psychology: risk reversal, the endowment effect (5 frames in your hands at home), fair-price anchoring.
   - Results: the brand says it hit first-year goals within weeks **(company-reported, unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warby_Parker
   - Lesson: letting people hold or try the product before committing converts.
2. **Specsavers "Should've gone to Specsavers," 2002 onward. Medium: TV, print.**
   - Psychology: humor about the consequences of poor eyesight, a mild fear of embarrassment, a catchphrase.
   - Results: a long-running UK catchphrase. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specsavers
   - Lesson: a funny "what goes wrong if you don't" works as an emotion-neutral way to show consequences.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-558, MMR-043.

### Plastic surgery
1. **Dove "Campaign for Real Beauty," 2004 onward. Medium: multichannel.** This is a counter-reference for aesthetic verticals.
   - Psychology: self-esteem, authenticity, in-group.
   - Results: the reported sales growth is **unverified**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove_Campaign_for_Real_Beauty
   - Lesson: confidence-focused outcomes ("feel like yourself again") rather than shame.
   - Compliance: before/after photos must be typical or disclosed (FTC Endorsement Guides). Some states regulate cosmetic surgery ads and require board certification claims to be accurate.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-555, MMR-200.

### Mammography & lung screening, urgent/primary care, vein care, travel medicine/vaccination, veterinary, sleep apnea
- No consumer campaign with verified results for these. Mailer evidence: screening MMR-598 (Lung Screen Uptake Trial, RCT), MMR-564; urgent/primary care MMR-248, MMR-303; vein care MMR-268; vaccination MMR-508, MMR-226; veterinary MMR-023, MMR-310; sleep apnea MMR-162.

**Patterns (health):** (1) Fear plus one clear action is proven (CDC Tips). Fear without an action backfires. (2) Correct a misconception or name an outside enemy. (3) Risk reversal and self-efficacy ("anyone can", "try at home"). HIPAA and FTC substantiation always apply.

## H. Insurance

### Final expense / life
1. **Colonial Penn "$9.95 a month" guaranteed-acceptance TV (Alex Trebek, later Jimmy Walker), 2010s. Medium: TV, direct response.**
   - Psychology: a low anchor price, "guaranteed acceptance" removes the fear of rejection, a trusted celebrity, fear of burdening family.
   - Results: a long-running DR program **(no public verified results)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Penn
   - Lesson: removing the rejection fear plus a small "unit" price is the core of final-expense offers.
   - Compliance, the reason it's here: the per-unit pricing and the graded death benefit (often 2 years) must be disclosed, and the format has drawn criticism. State insurance-ad regulations (NAIC Model 570) and senior-protection rules apply. Mando flagged final expense as *negative examples only*.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-047, MMR-530, MMR-437.

### Workers' comp
- No verified campaign. Mailer evidence: MMR-270, MMR-554; attorney MMR-628.

**Patterns:** (1) Remove the fear of rejection. (2) Small unit pricing is powerful and heavily regulated. (3) Protecting the family is the outcome to sell.

## I. Hospitality & entertainment

1. **Tourism Queensland "The Best Job in the World," 2009. Medium: online recruitment stunt.**
   - Psychology: curiosity, aspiration, a contest, scarcity (one job), user-generated content.
   - Results: about 34,000 video applications from about 200 countries, and large PR value claims **(claimant-reported, PR value unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Job_in_the_World
   - Lesson: a contest hook gets a card read and talked about. Sweepstakes laws apply (no purchase necessary, official rules).
2. **Las Vegas "What Happens Here, Stays Here" (R&R Partners for the LVCVA), 2003. Medium: TV.**
   - Psychology: permission and license, a secret in-group, freedom (escape).
   - Results: a long-running tagline **(visitation attribution unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Convention_and_Visitors_Authority
   - Lesson: sell the emotional outcome, escape and permission, not amenities.
3. **Motel 6 "We'll leave the light on for you" (Tom Bodett), 1986. Medium: radio.**
   - Psychology: warmth and liking, a plain-spoken voice, self-deprecating value positioning.
   - Results: one of the longest-running radio campaigns. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motel_6
   - Lesson: a homespun voice makes a budget offer feel caring rather than cheap.
4. **Vail Resorts Epic Pass, 2008. Medium: pricing/product.**
   - Psychology: pre-commitment, sunk cost, a bundle anchor, a deadline (prices rise after a date).
   - Results: reshaped ski-industry pricing toward season passes bought in advance. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Pass
   - Lesson: "lock in this price before [date]" sells season memberships, from ski passes to shooting ranges.
- Mailer evidence: hotels and resorts MMR-545, MMR-605, MMR-035; cruise MMR-376, MMR-467; tours MMR-439; comedy MMR-493; shooting range MMR-179; kids' camps and attractions MMR-556. No verified campaigns for cruise, comedy, shooting ranges, sports tickets or kids' camps specifically.

**Patterns:** (1) Sell escape and permission, the outcome. (2) Pre-commitment with price-rise deadlines. (3) Contests and stunts create talkability, and sweepstakes law applies.

## J. Weddings (wedding vendors / formalwear / jewelry-adjacent)
1. **De Beers "A Diamond Is Forever" (N.W. Ayer), 1947. Medium: print, then all media.**
   - Psychology: symbolism (permanence equals love), a social norm creation, an anchoring rule like "two months' salary" (added later).
   - Results: often credited with making diamond engagement rings the norm. Share statistics are commonly quoted but **unverified here**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Beers
   - Lesson: attach the product to a life milestone's meaning.
   - Mailer evidence: MMR-038 (wedding vendors), MMR-494 (formalwear).

## K. B2B
1. **Salesforce "No Software," 1999. Medium: guerrilla stunts, PR, a logo.**
   - Psychology: enemy framing (legacy on-premise software), the distinctive "no" symbol, contrast.
   - Results: grew into a category leader **(attribution unverified)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesforce
   - Lesson: B2B mail can name the old way as the enemy.
- Mailer evidence: SaaS MMR-358; IT/MSP MMR-541; staffing MMR-086, MMR-153; equipment auctions MMR-497, MMR-615. No verified campaigns for IT services, staffing or auctions.

## L. Litter / civic behavior (an analogue for "neighborhood pride" home-service cards)
1. **"Don't Mess with Texas" (TxDOT, GSD&M), 1986. Medium: TV, road signs.**
   - Psychology: identity and regional pride. It recast littering as un-Texan to reach the target group of young men.
   - Results: TxDOT has reported a large drop in roadside litter in the first years **(agency-reported, unverified magnitude)**. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Mess_with_Texas
   - Lesson: "Proud [town] homeowners keep their [yards/roofs] sharp." Local identity is the persuader.
