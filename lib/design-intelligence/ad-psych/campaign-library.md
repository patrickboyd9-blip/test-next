# Campaign Library: Psychologically Effective Ads by Vertical
Modern Mail, research for the AI copywriter and art director. Prepared Oct 20, 2026.

> **BASELINE CRITERIA (hard rule from Patrick):** every mailer the AI generates or recommends must offer the client a sell-the-outcome option (principle #21). We are emotion-neutral: fear and joy are equally valid. The only hard limits are law and compliance.

**How to read this file**
- Principle numbers (#1–#21) refer to `postcard-principles.md`. Emotion and need terms refer to `emotion-needs-madmen-report.md`.
- **Results:** a number is listed only when a source was found this session. **(unverified: company/agency claim)** means the only source is the brand, its agency or a vendor. Award wins are noted as such. "No verified results found" means we found none and did not guess.
- **We did not pad.** Several trade verticals have fewer than 5 documented campaigns, and each one is flagged.

## 1. Roofing (3 documented; we found no 5th or 4th roofing-brand campaign with public results)
**1. Owens Corning Roofing: Pink Panther spots** (Pink Panther used since 1980; roofing spot published 2018) · TV/online video
- Core idea: a beloved mascot owns the category's color ("pink" = Owens Corning).
- Psychology: mere exposure and repetition (#19), liking (#8), nostalgia, Von Restorff (#14), distinctive color (#13).
- Results: no verified results found for the roofing spots. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jy9F9Ynl8E ; related 2024 insulation revival: https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/build-with-pink
- Lesson for postcards: use one consistent brand asset (color, mascot, face) on every drop so card #3 is recognized at a glance.

**2. Owens Corning Roofing: "Roof Smarter" + "Roof Gawking"** · digital, video, influencers (#RoofLove)
- Core idea: homeowners scout their neighbors' roofs for ideas, so the campaign made roof color an exciting design choice and taught people how to manage the project.
- Psychology: social proof (#3), sell the outcome (curb appeal, #21), authority through education (#4), turning a grudge purchase into anticipation/joy.
- Results: 889K impressions, 337.9K video views **(unverified: agency claim)**. Source: https://interruptdelivers.com/work/owens-corning-roofing/homeowner-marketing
- Lesson for postcards: show the finished house on the street ("what your home could look like"), not the shingle.

**3. Atlas Roofing: HP42 "BFD (Bigger Faster Design)" launch** (2018) · social (aimed at contractors)
- Core idea: a cheeky double-meaning headline plus contractor testimonials.
- Psychology: humor/curiosity (#9), peer social proof (#3), liking (#8).
- Results: won Hanley Wood's 2018 Gold Brand Builder Award; post reach figures **(unverified: agency claim)**. Source: https://trustworkz.com/blog/2019/03/21/trustworkz-and-atlas-roofing-win-gold-for-hp42-product-launch/
- Lesson for postcards: a witty, memorable headline earns a second look, and a local customer quote supplies the proof.

*(See also Origin Energy "Rate My Roof" under Solar.)*

**Patterns**
- Roofing brands win by turning a grudge purchase into curb appeal and pride (sell the outcome).
- Distinctive, repeated brand assets beat one-off clever ads.
- Neighbor and peer proof matters: people really do look at the street.

## 2. Windows (3 documented)
**1. Renewal by Andersen: "Say Anything" parody** (Hatfield Media, ~2024–25) · digital video
- Core idea: the boombox serenade is silenced the moment she closes her Renewal window.
- Psychology: humor, nostalgia/pop-culture reference, demonstrating the benefit (quiet), sell the outcome (#21).
- Results: won a Regional Emmy (Ohio Valley chapter); no sales data. Source: https://hatfieldmedia.com/our-work/renewal-by-andersen-emmy-award-winning-brand-campaign
- Lesson for postcards: dramatize ONE felt benefit (quiet, warmth, lower bills) in one image with a twist.

**2. Integrity Windows & Doors from Marvin: "Carl and Stu Skills Challenge"** (2010) · viral video, print, banners, **direct mail**, microsite
- Core idea: trick-shot builders show off their skills, and the brand rides along.
- Psychology: curiosity (#9), entertainment/liking (#8), participation (commitment, #7).
- Results: 7M+ online views and an Adweek 2010 BUZZ Award **(views figure: agency press release)**. Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/martinwilliams-receives-buzz-award-for-integrity-windows-and-doors-viral-marketing-campaign-95050389.html
- Lesson for postcards: a card can point to a short video or challenge with a QR code. Entertainment earns the attention.

**3. Pella: "Windows for People"** (2016) · webisodes, TV, social, print
- Core idea: windows that meet real, quirky human needs, not specs.
- Psychology: sell the outcome (#21), liking and relatability (#8).
- Results: 2016 Des Moines ADDY Award; no sales data. Source: https://www.januaryholmes.com/pella
- Lesson for postcards: lead with the person and their life in the room, not with U-values.

**Patterns**
- Every documented window win sells a felt outcome (quiet, light, life) rather than specs.
- Humor and pop-culture twists are the main attention tool.
- Results data is thin in this category, so A/B test on mail.

## 3. Solar (4 documented)
**1. Sunrun: "Brilliant"** (Cutwater) · TV/digital
- Core idea: everything in your home can run on the sun, and choosing that is "brilliant".
- Psychology: identity and self-esteem (Maslow esteem: "you're the smart one"), joy/optimism, sell the outcome (#21). Insight: buyers saw solar as progress and a hedge against rising costs, not just savings.
- Results: "doubled brand awareness" **(unverified: agency claim)**. Source: https://www.cutwateragency.com/sunrun
- Lesson for postcards: flatter the decision ("the smart move on your street") as well as the savings.

**2. Sunrun: YouTube TrueView campaign** (2014) · online video
- Core idea: a light, fun video that creates the need for solar service.
- Psychology: curiosity (#9), humor.
- Results: 101% lift in ad recall vs. control (Google Brand Lift); 1.8M views; 215% more site visits in the target area (client-provided). Source: https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/500/sunrun-shines-light-on-campaign-results_case-studies.pdf
- Lesson for postcards: a fun tone still works in a "serious money" category.

**3. SunPower: "Demand Better Solar"** (Pitch, ~2016) · TV, print, radio, digital, social, outdoor
- Core idea: NASA and solar-airplane credentials show that not all solar is equal.
- Psychology: authority (#4), quality differentiation. Adweek's critique: the ad hinted at its record efficiency claim instead of stating it, a lesson in specificity (#15).
- Results: none published. Source: https://www.adweek.com/agencyspy/pitch-sunpower-want-you-to-demand-better-solar/
- Lesson for postcards: if you hold a true, provable claim, state it plainly and specifically.

**4. Origin Energy (Australia): "Rate My Roof"** (Clemenger BBDO, 2016) · mobile/web tool
- Core idea: draw your own roof on your phone to see your solar savings.
- Psychology: personalization (#18), endowment and IKEA effect (#17), curiosity (#9), self-generated proof.
- Results: Cannes Lions Bronze 2016; One Show Interactive Gold Pencil. Sources: https://adsspot.me/media/case-study/origin-energy-rate-my-roof-4-80dc966d169f ; https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-credit/2208/2016/all/1
- Lesson for postcards: print the reader's own roof or address with a personalized estimate ("Your roof could produce ~X"), using verified data only.

**Patterns**
- Personalization ("your roof, your number") is the strongest documented mechanic.
- Identity and optimism ("brilliant") pair with the money hedge.
- Compliance: savings claims need substantiation (FTC Act Section 5). Solar has had state and FTC scrutiny over misleading savings and "free" claims (general note; we did not research a specific case this session).

## 4. Gutters (4 documented)
**1. LeafFilter: first national TV/digital video campaign** (Hawthorne, 2018–) · TV, online video
- Core idea: safety (stay off the ladder), trust, and testimonials.
- Psychology: fear of injury plus easy action (fear appeal + efficacy), social proof (#3), authority ("#1 rated").
- Results: 160M+ impressions in 8 weeks (67% over goal); hit the yearly sales target on a third of the budget; 200% YoY rise in TV-attributed leads; 13% lower cost per lead **(unverified: agency claims)**. Source: https://www.hawthorneadvertising.com/work/leaffilter/
- Lesson for postcards: show the danger (ladder) and the relief (never clean gutters again) together.

**2. LeafGuard (Midwest dealers): "Cranky Squirrel"** · broadcast, digital
- Core idea: a wry, urbane squirrel who doesn't want his roof messed with.
- Psychology: humor, mascot liking (#8), mere exposure (#19).
- Results: expanded to 9 markets; lower cost per lead; some markets grew up to 30% in a year **(unverified: consultant claim)**. Source: https://www.titletownmarketing.com/case-studies/creative/
- Lesson for postcards: a local mascot makes a commodity service memorable across repeated drops.

**3. LeafGuard on Nextdoor: "Stay Off Your Ladders" / "Take Your Time Back"** · neighborhood social
- Core idea: test safety framing against time-saving framing by region.
- Psychology: fear/safety vs. sell-the-outcome (time back); local social context (#3).
- Results: best creative 13% below its CPA goal; "Take Your Time Back" $157.64 CPA (West NY); "Stay Off Your Ladders" $189.86 CPA (S. Georgia) **(platform case study)**. Source: https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/resources/success-stories/leafguard-gives-nextdoor-neighbors-a-reason-to-stay-off-their-ladders
- Lesson for postcards: **fear and outcome framing both work, and which wins depends on the market**. That is direct support for the emotion-neutral stance. Offer both versions and split-test them.

**4. Gutter Helmet of Central Indiana: "Sammy Terry" Halloween campaign** (Onya) · social video
- Core idea: a beloved local horror-TV host narrates "gutter nightmares".
- Psychology: playful fear, local nostalgia/liking, limited-time offer (#10).
- Results: 140,385 impressions; 43,544 engagements; 1,375 clicks; **only 3 conversions** **(agency claim)**. Source: https://www.onyamark.com/gutter-helmet-case-study/
- Lesson for postcards: engagement is not leads. Fun creative still needs a strong, simple offer and call to action.

*Also relevant:* LeafGuard's media-mix modeling found **direct mail**, Facebook, TV and YouTube were undervalued by last-click attribution (CAC down 12%) **(vendor claim)**. Source: https://tinuiti.com/work/omnichannel-case-study-leafguard/

**Patterns**
- Two proven framings are safety/fear (ladder falls, water damage) and the outcome (time back, never clean gutters). Test both.
- Mascots and local celebrities build memory, but conversion needs an offer.
- Mail is an undervalued upper-funnel driver in this category.

## 5. Auto Insurance (4 documented)
**1. GEICO: the Gecko + "15 minutes could save you 15% or more"** (1999–) · TV, all media
- Core idea: a likable mascot plus one memorable, specific promise.
- Psychology: liking (#8), mere exposure (#19), specificity (#15), loss aversion ("save").
- Results: MarketCast brand-effect research shows the Gecko +3% above norm on recall. Source: https://marketcast.com/news/marketcast-brand-effect-research-reveals-the-most-effective-insurance-mascots-in-advertising/
- Lesson for postcards: one short, specific, repeatable promise.

**2. Progressive: Flo** (2008–) · TV, all media
- Core idea: a cheerful clerk in an all-white "store" makes insurance feel like simple retail.
- Psychology: liking, consistency, simplicity (fluency).
- Results: +8% message recall, +6% branding vs. norm (MarketCast). Same source as above.
- Lesson for postcards: make a complex product feel like a simple purchase.

**3. Allstate: "Mayhem"** (Leo Burnett, 2010–) · TV, all media
- Core idea: Mayhem personifies the accidents that cheap insurance won't cover.
- Psychology: **fear plus humor**, loss aversion (#6), vivid "what could go wrong" stories, then the protector (Allstate).
- Results: leads ad recall, likeability and memorability; +5% recall, +8% likeability, +7% messaging vs. norm (MarketCast). Same source.
- Lesson for postcards: personify the threat (hail, the leak, the storm) with humor so fear doesn't repel, and then present the brand as the fix.

**4. State Farm: "Jake from State Farm"** (2011 viral; 2020 recast) · TV, social
- Core idea: a "good neighbor" agent as a real person.
- Psychology: liking and trust, the familiar-face effect.
- Results: no verified numbers found this session.
- Lesson for postcards: a real, consistent local face builds trust.

*Caution:* UCLA Anderson research found humorous auto-insurance ads build awareness but may not change quote shopping much. Source: https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/insurance-advertising-honka/

**Patterns**
- Mascots and repetition dominate, so memory is the battlefield.
- Fear works best wrapped in humor (Mayhem).
- Awareness is not switching: pair the creative with a concrete savings reason.

## 6. Health Insurance (4 documented)
**1. Aflac: the Duck** (2000–) · TV
- Core idea: a duck shouting the brand name fixes "what's an Aflac?"
- Psychology: mere exposure, humor, name repetition (Von Restorff, #14).
- Results: Effie case; agency says the company grew from $4B to $11B and awareness jumped **(unverified: agency claim)**. Sources: https://effie.org/cases/aflac-duck/ ; https://www.tomanderic911.com/aflac-1
- Lesson for postcards: if the brand name is obscure, make the name itself the hook.

**2. Oscar Health: NYC launch** (2014) · subway outdoor, print
- Core idea: plain-talk, friendly ads against confusing insurance jargon.
- Psychology: underdog positioning (David vs. Goliath), cognitive ease, liking.
- Results: Silver Effie (David vs. Goliath) and Gold Effie (design); 45% core awareness; 5% NYC share; 15,000 sign-ups **(per designer portfolio, citing Effie)**. Source: https://www.mariannepizzi.com/work/oscar
- Lesson for postcards: plain English is a competitive weapon in jargon-heavy categories.

**3. Kaiser Permanente: "Thrive"** (2004–) · TV, print
- Core idea: sell health and living well, not plans.
- Psychology: sell the outcome (#21), optimism, Maslow self-actualization.
- Results: statistically significant gains in awareness and favorability **(per consultant PDF)**. Source: https://www.masonstrategicmarketing.com/_files/ugd/f1d272_00619d24aa044d4f8351f003e2651681.pdf?index=true
- Lesson for postcards: an aspirational outcome can carry an entire brand.

**4. UnitedHealthcare: "Ways In"** · multi-media
- Results: 2017 North American Effie Gold, Healthcare Services. Source: https://www.effieinsights.com/en/campaigns/unitedhealthcare%3A-ways-in-b57b86e1ecd9469bbe1ed90052beeec2 (We did not read the case details.)

**Patterns**
- Simplicity and plain talk beat jargon.
- The outcome (thriving, peace of mind) is what sells.
- Name recognition by repetition works when awareness is low.

## 7. Medicare (2 documented: fewer than 5. Most Medicare TV is lead-gen, and its documented "results" are regulatory actions.)
**1. Joe Namath / "Medicare Coverage Helpline" TV ads** (~2019–2023) · TV
- Core idea: a celebrity says "you may be missing out on benefits", then gives a 1-800 number.
- Psychology: authority/celebrity (#4), loss aversion/FOMO (#6), urgency.
- Compliance: the **Senate Finance Committee 2022 report** criticized these ads for leaving out limits (ZIP-code availability, networks). **CMS** then tightened rules: ads must name specific plans, can't misuse Medicare branding, and must disclose availability. Sources: https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/Deceptive%20Marketing%20Practices%20Flourish%20in%20Medicare%20Advantage.pdf ; https://www.aarp.org/medicare/feds-crack-down-on-misleading-medicare-advantage-ads/ ; https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/hhs-proposes-rule-strengthen-beneficiary-protections-improve-access-behavioral-health-care-promote
- Results: no verified response data (marketing complaints more than doubled 2020→2021, per the Senate report).
- Lesson for postcards: FOMO and authority clearly pull response, but every Medicare card must pass our CMS checklist (plan names, disclaimers, no government look-alike design, no "free" for $0 premium).

**2. Humana: "The Whole You"** · digital, direct mail, outdoor, TV/radio
- Core idea: co-branded stories from local providers about whole-person health.
- Psychology: local authority and trust (#4, #8), sell the outcome (#21).
- Results: none published. Source: https://heinrich.com/work/web-digital/the-whole-you-humana/
- Lesson for postcards: a local doctor or clinic partner beats a celebrity on trust, and it's easier to keep compliant.

**Patterns**
- Celebrity plus "missing benefits" FOMO is the dominant TV formula, and it is now a regulatory target.
- Local, trusted messengers are the compliant alternative.
- In this category, compliance is part of the creative.

## 8. Debt Consolidation (2 campaigns + 2 enforcement cases: fewer than 5)
**1. Payoff (Happy Money): "Fund Your Happy"** · digital/brand
- Core idea: the emotional goal of being debt-free, not interest rates.
- Psychology: sell the outcome (#21), relief/joy, Maslow safety.
- Results: 51% YoY revenue growth (Feb 2022) **(unverified: portfolio claim)**. Source: https://www.jessecalane.com/happy-money
- Lesson for postcards: show life after debt, not APRs.

**2. SoFi: student-loan refinance savings ads** (2016–2019) · TV, print, online
- Core idea: "average savings $X".
- Psychology: specificity (#15), anchoring.
- Compliance: **FTC 2019 consent order**. The "average" savings figure was selectively calculated. SoFi now must have competent, reliable evidence for savings claims. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/162-3197-social-finance-inc-sofi-lending-corp-matter
- Lesson for postcards: specific savings numbers persuade, so they must be honestly calculated and documented.

**Enforcement example (not a campaign):** FTC 2025 action against a debt-relief operation that impersonated government and businesses. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-halts-illegal-debt-relief-operation-falsely-impersonated-businesses-government-harming-consumers. The Telemarketing Sales Rule also bans upfront fees for debt relief.

**Patterns**
- Relief and freedom (outcome) is the emotional core.
- Numbers drive response, and they also drive FTC actions.
- Never imply a government program.

## 9. Credit Repair / Credit (4 documented)
**1. Experian: FreeCreditReport.com "band" jingles** (2007–2010) · TV, online
- Core idea: catchy singing-guy jingles about bad credit wrecking your life.
- Psychology: humor plus fear of social embarrassment, earworm repetition (#19).
- Compliance: the **FTC amended the Free Credit Reports Rule (2010)** to require prominent disclosures pointing to AnnualCreditReport.com, and the band was retired. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2010/02/ftc-amends-free-credit-reports-rule-help-consumers-steer-clear-free-offers-cost-money ; https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/experian-says-goodbye-freecreditreportcom-band-107398/
- Lesson for postcards: "free" is legally loaded. Only say it if it's truly free.

**2. Experian Boost launch** (2019–) · app-store and digital
- Core idea: instant points from bills you already pay.
- Psychology: quick win (endowed progress, #11), gain framing, specificity.
- Results: 6x increase in branded app downloads (Apple Ads case study); Experian says users averaged a 12-point FICO increase **(company claim)**. Sources: https://ads.apple.com/app-store/success-stories/experian ; https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/experian-boost-study/
- Lesson for postcards: offer a small, fast, concrete first step.

**3. Credit Karma: "free credit scores"** · TV, digital
- Psychology: reciprocity through a free tool (#2), curiosity about your score (#9).
- Results: none verified this session. Listed as an illustration only.

**4. Lexington Law / CreditRepair.com (Progrexion)** · affiliate online and telemarketing
- Compliance (key cautionary case): **CFPB** found illegal upfront fees and affiliate bait ads ("guarantee ANYONE a 0–3.5% down home loan"). 2023 settlement: $2.7B judgment and a 10-year telemarketing ban. Sources: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-reaches-multibillion-dollar-settlement-with-credit-repair-conglomerate/ ; https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_pgx-holdings_complaint_2019-05.pdf
- Lesson for postcards: no guarantees of results or loans. Credit Repair Organizations Act and TSR rules on fees apply.

**Patterns**
- Curiosity about "your number" and quick wins drive response.
- Fear of embarrassment works (the jingles), but "free" claims are regulated.
- This is the highest-enforcement category we studied, so legal review of client claims is essential.

## 10. Restaurants (5 documented)
**1. Domino's: "Pizza Turnaround"** (2009–2010) · TV, online documentary
- Core idea: admit the pizza was bad, show the fix, and show real critics.
- Psychology: radical honesty and vulnerability (trust), the underdog, the pratfall effect.
- Results: Q4 2009 global retail sales +16.4% (Domino's SEC press release; also credits the new recipe). Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1286681/000119312510045329/dex991.htm
- Lesson for postcards: a candid "here's what we fixed" message builds trust.

**2. Burger King: "Whopper Detour"** (FCB NY, 2018) · app, geofencing
- Core idea: get a 1-cent Whopper, but only when you're near a McDonald's.
- Psychology: rivalry/humor, extreme price anchor (#15), gamification, scarcity (#5).
- Results: 1.5M app downloads; 3.5B earned impressions; 37:1 ROI; Cannes Direct and Titanium Grand Prix **(results via agency/Cannes entry)**. Source: https://www.adweek.com/creativity/whopper-detour-burger-kings-masterpiece-of-trolling-wins-direct-grand-prix-at-cannes/
- Lesson for postcards: a provocative, playful offer mechanic can outperform a plain discount.

**3. Popeyes: Chicken Sandwich launch / Twitter reply** (2019)
- Core idea: a one-line "y'all good?" reply to Chick-fil-A turned into a sellout frenzy.
- Psychology: scarcity (#5; it sold out), social proof (#3), rivalry, FOMO.
- Results: ~$65M estimated earned media value in 15 days (third-party estimate reported by Forbes). Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/michelinemaynard/2019/08/28/the-popeyes-chicken-sandwich-now-sold-out-is-a-65-million-marketing-win/
- Lesson for postcards: real scarcity ("back for 2 weeks only") creates urgency, but only when it's true.

**4. Wendy's: "Where's the Beef?"** (1984) · TV
- Core idea: elderly Clara Peller demands meat, mocking rivals' small patties.
- Psychology: humor, comparison advertising, a catchphrase.
- Results: well-documented cultural phenomenon; no verified sales figure this session. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_the_beef%3F
- Lesson for postcards: a short, quotable line that frames the comparison.

**5. Taco Bell: "Steal a Base, Steal a Taco"** (2007–) · TV/sports promotion
- Core idea: a stolen base in the World Series means a free taco for America.
- Psychology: reciprocity (#2), anticipation/game, community event.
- Results: millions claimed free tacos; no sales impact disclosed. Source: https://frontofficesports.com/taco-bell-world-series-advertising/
- Lesson for postcards: tie the offer to a local event (a home team win = a deal).

**Patterns**
- Honesty, rivalry and humor build buzz.
- Scarcity and game mechanics drive visits.
- The offer mechanic is creative too.

## 11. Food Coupons / Promotions (3 documented + cross-listed)
**1. McDonald's: Monopoly** (1987–) · in-store game pieces, online codes
- Core idea: collect game pieces to win prizes.
- Psychology: endowed progress / Zeigarnik (#11: "one piece to go"), variable rewards, anticipation.
- Results: 2010 run had a 5.6% YoY sales lift and 30M online code entries in 42 days (IMA award). Source: https://www.chiefmarketer.com/2010-monopoly-at-mcdonalds-2011-ima-award-winner/
- Compliance: the 1989–2001 prize-fraud scandal (an insider stole winning pieces) shows sweepstakes need audited controls and official rules. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald%27s_Monopoly
- Lesson for postcards: a collect-and-complete mechanic (stamp cards, "3 of 4 punches") drives repeat visits. Sweepstakes laws apply.

**2. Taco Bell: Steal a Base, Steal a Taco** (see Restaurants #5): a free-item promotion tied to an event.

**3. Burger King: Whopper Detour** (see Restaurants #2): a 1-cent coupon with a playful catch.

**Patterns**
- Games and progress mechanics beat flat discounts for repeat visits.
- Free or near-free anchors create buzz.
- Contests and sweepstakes carry legal requirements.

## Cross-Vertical Takeaways
1. **Sell the outcome everywhere:** curb appeal, quiet, time back, relief, the life in the home. Every top campaign leads with the after, not the spec. This confirms our baseline rule.
2. **Fear and joy both win, and context decides.** LeafGuard's Nextdoor test had safety winning in one market and time-saving in another. Allstate wraps fear in humor. Offer the client both versions and split-test.
3. **Memory assets compound.** Mascots (Gecko, Mayhem, Duck, squirrel), colors (pink) and catchphrases win on repetition. Mail series should reuse one asset on every card.
4. **Personalization and mechanics beat flat claims.** Rate My Roof, Monopoly and Whopper Detour turned the offer into an interaction.
5. **Honesty is persuasive.** Domino's, "Ugly Houses" and Oscar won by plain, candid talk.
6. **Compliance sits next to the strongest pulls.** "Free", savings numbers, guarantees, government look-alikes and Medicare FOMO are exactly where the FTC, CFPB and CMS acted. Persuasive claims must be true and documented.
7. **Engagement isn't conversion.** Every card needs one clear offer and one action.
