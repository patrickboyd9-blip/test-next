# How Emotion, Needs & Ad History Will Shape Modern Mail Postcards

> **BASELINE CRITERIA (hard rule from Patrick):** every mailer the AI generates or recommends must always offer the client a sell-the-outcome option or guidance (benefits and experience over features, as in principle #21). This applies whatever emotion is chosen, fear or joy alike.

*Executive brief, Oct 20, 2026. Full sources are in research-notes.md.*

**Bottom line:** People buy on emotion and justify the purchase with logic. Each postcard should pick **one main emotion (the client's choice; the AI favors none)**, **one core need**, and **one awareness level**, then back them with a specific, true reason to act now.

---
## 1. Plutchik's Wheel of Emotions
Eight primary emotions (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation) at three intensities, plus blends such as **Love** (joy+trust), **Optimism** (anticipation+joy) and **Awe** (fear+surprise). Source: Plutchik 1980; *American Scientist* 2001.
**AI rules**
- Copywriter: tag each card with one target emotion and keep the headline, image and call-to-action consistent with it. The client chooses the emotion. The AI presents options and favors none (see the table below).
- Intensity is the client's choice. Whatever the emotion, it has to rest on true facts (see Compliance Limits).

**Emotion options by vertical (no default)**
| Vertical | Positive-emotion options | Fear/negative-emotion options |
|---|---|---|
| Roofing | Love/protect family, Trust, Pride (curb appeal) | Fear of storm damage, leaks or a denied insurance claim |
| We-buy-houses | Relief, Optimism / fresh start | Fear of losing home equity, foreclosure timelines, repair costs |
| Medicare | Trust, Serenity, Confidence | Fear of coverage gaps or out-of-pocket costs (must stay accurate and not misleading under CMS rules) |
| Coupons / food | Joy, Anticipation, Smart-shopper pride | Fear of missing out on a real deadline, deprivation ("Got Milk?") |
- Art director: imagery has to carry the same emotion. Wheel colors are only a starting point because the evidence on color is weak. A/B test them.
- ✅ **Do (roofing):** "Keep the people you love warm and dry this winter," with a photo of a family by the window and a local crew's faces.
- ✅ **Also a Do (fear option):** "Last week's hail hit [Neighborhood]. Hidden damage can turn into leaks. Free 20-minute inspection: call by [real date]." This is true, specific and gives a clear action.
- ❌ **Don't:** "YOUR ROOF WILL COLLAPSE TONIGHT." It's an unprovable claim and could be deceptive under FTC Act §5. The problem is the false claim, not the fear.

## 2. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs
Maslow (1943) named physiological, safety, belonging, esteem and self-actualization needs, and said the order is flexible. **He never drew the pyramid** (a consultant drew it in 1960). Research doesn't support a strict ladder, and Kenrick et al. (2010) added **family and parenting**.
**AI rules**
- Map each offer to a main need and a supporting need: food = physiological + belonging; roofing = safety + family; Medicare = security + esteem (independence); we-buy-houses = security + fresh start.
- Never assume people "climb" the ladder. A struggling seller still wants dignity (esteem).
- ✅ **Do (we-buy-houses):** "Sell as-is, on your timeline, and start your next chapter," covering security plus dignity.
- ❌ **Don't:** "Your bank has scheduled your foreclosure," sent without verified facts, or anything that falsely implies a lender or government connection (FTC §5; 39 USC 3001).

## 3. Mad Men (Fact vs. Fiction)
Sterling Cooper and Don's pitches are fiction. Kodak's Carousel projector (launched 1961) and Lucky Strike's "It's Toasted" (used since about 1917) are real, but Don didn't create them. The real revolution came from Bernbach's DDB ("Think Small," 1959; Avis "We Try Harder," 1962) and David Ogilvy, a direct-response devotee.
**AI rules**
- **Nostalgia (Carousel):** for home and senior audiences, evoke memories of the home ("the house where the kids grew up"). Research shows nostalgia lowers how much people focus on price (Lasaleta et al. 2014).
- **Own a true claim (Lucky Strike):** feature something competitors also do but don't talk about, such as "Every roof gets a free 21-point inspection," **only if it's true**.
- **Ogilvy:** the headline carries the card. Be specific (numbers, street names), caption photos, and test.
- **Think Small:** white space and honest, plain talk beat clutter.
- ✅ **Do (Medicare):** "Questions about your Medicare options? Talk to a licensed agent who lives in [Town]," with a clean layout, a real photo and a caption.
- ❌ **Don't:** cram it with starbursts and vague superlatives ("BEST DEALS EVER!!!").

## 4. Famous Ad Phenomena → Mailer Plays
| Phenomenon | Play for postcards |
|---|---|
| "Got Milk?" deprivation | Show life *without* the product: a wet ceiling stain, an empty fridge |
| Share a Coke | Variable-data personalization: name, street, a photo of the home |
| IKEA effect (Norton et al. 2012) | Let them take part: "Scan to pick your shingle color / build your plan" |
| Avis "We Try Harder" | Small local business? Own it: "We answer our own phone" |
| Pepsi Challenge | Demonstration and comparison, done fairly and with proof |
| Think Different / Dove | Identity and real people over stock models |
| Where's the Beef? | A memorable, humorous comparison hook (coupons, food) |
| WSJ "Two Young Men" (Conroy) | A story-led back side: "Two neighbors on your street…" |
| Schwartz's awareness levels | Cold lists lead with the problem or story; customer lists lead with the offer |
| Bernays / De Beers | Identity and ritual framing. Note that health claims like those in the tobacco campaigns would now face FTC substantiation rules |
- ✅ **Do (coupons):** "Ran out of dinner ideas? Tacos for 4, $5 off—tonight," using deprivation, joy and a real deadline.
- ❌ **Don't:** "Only 3 coupons left in your area!" when that isn't true (deceptive scarcity).

## 5. Fear Appeals: What the Science Says
- **Rogers (1975), protection motivation theory:** people act when they see a threat as serious and likely, *and* believe the fix works and that they can do it.
- **Witte (1992), Extended Parallel Process Model (EPPM):** when threat is high and efficacy is high, people act to control the danger. When threat is high and efficacy is low, they control their fear instead (denial, tossing the card).
- **Tannenbaum et al. (2015), meta-analysis of 127 articles:** fear appeals are effective overall. They work best when the message includes efficacy statements and recommends a one-time action.
- **Rule for the AI:** if a card uses fear, pair it with a specific, easy, one-time action (call, scan, book a free inspection).

| Vertical | ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Roofing | "Hail hit [ZIP] on [date]. Many insurers set claim deadlines; check your policy. Book a free inspection: [phone]." | Threat with no action, or invented damage claims ("We saw damage on your roof" when nobody inspected it) |
| We-buy-houses | "Repairs and holding costs can eat into your equity. Get a no-obligation cash offer in 24 hours." | False foreclosure notices, or a design that looks like a court or lender document |
| Medicare | "Does your plan cover your doctors and prescriptions? A licensed agent can compare your options. [TTY/hours]" | Inaccurate claims about losing benefits; implying CMS or Medicare sent the card |
| Coupons | "Offer ends Sunday [real date]. Don't miss $5 off." | Fake countdowns or a false "only 3 left" |

## 6. John B. Watson: Behaviorism Goes to Madison Avenue
- He founded behaviorism with "Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It" (1913) and argued that **fear, rage and love** are the three innate emotions (Watson & Morgan 1917).
- **Little Albert (Watson & Rayner 1920)** conditioned an infant to fear a white rat. It has serious problems: one child, no consent standards, Albert was never deconditioned, and replication was poor. Even who "Albert" was is still disputed (Beck et al. 2009; Fridlund et al. 2012; Powell et al. 2014).
- **Ad career:** he joined J. Walter Thompson in 1920 and later became a vice president. He worked on Pond's, Maxwell House, Johnson's Baby Powder, Pebeco and Odorono (Buckley 1982; *The New Yorker* 1928). **Myths to avoid:** he did *not* invent the "coffee break" (the term was popularized in the 1950s by the Pan-American Coffee Bureau), and he didn't invent testimonials (JWT ran Pond's celebrity testimonials from 1914, before he arrived).
- **For mailers:** fear, rage (frustration) and love are three practical lenses. Examples: fear of a leak, frustration with a slow agent, love of family. His habit of market research and segmenting by audience fits Modern Mail's data-driven targeting.

## 7. Compliance Limits (the only hard rules)
Modern Mail stays neutral about emotion. These are the legal lines:
- **Baseline criteria (always applies):** every generated or recommended mailer includes a sell-the-outcome option or guidance (benefits and experience over features, principle #21), whatever the emotion.
1. **FTC Act §5 (15 USC 45):** no deceptive or unfair claims. Every claim must be true and backed up, every deadline and scarcity claim real, and testimonials and endorsements must follow the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255).
2. **39 USC 3001 (USPS deceptive mailings):** no designs that look like government mail without the required disclaimers, and no fake checks or invoices.
3. **Medicare (42 CFR 422.2260–2274 / 423.2260–2274):** no misleading, inaccurate or confusing content; no implied CMS or Medicare endorsement; no "free" for a $0 premium; no targeting by health status or income; TTY numbers and hours required; TPMO disclaimer required; licensed agents and a Scope of Appointment required.
4. **Fair Housing Act (42 USC 3604(c)):** we-buy-houses and real estate mail can't state preferences based on a protected class, and imagery and targeting must not discriminate.
5. **State rules:** state insurance marketing rules (Medicare and insurance), state contractor and home-solicitation rules, and the FTC Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR Part 429) for in-home sales, which matters for roofing follow-ups. The client checks their state's rules.
6. **No fabricated data:** placeholders stay in place until the client supplies verified numbers, reviews or testimonials.
