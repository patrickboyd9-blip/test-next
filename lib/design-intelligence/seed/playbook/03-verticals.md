# Vertical guidance

These notes bias the principles toward a buyer. They are not licenses to copy a brand. Where the photographed corpus has a gap, the gap is stated.

Shared generation rule for every vertical: one audience moment (TP-12), one offer, one response lockup, documentary or result photography, no government cues, no fake deadline.

## Roofing

**Buyer blocker.** Trust and fear of a bad crew, often right after weather. The owner cannot see the roof from the curb in any useful way.

**Lead with.** A documentary photograph of a real roof, crew, or tear-off, full-bleed. Or an aerial that shows the whole roof plane. The offer is a concrete first step (a free inspection or a written check), set as a numeral if there is a price or a dollar-off.

**Palette and type.** Near-black or navy type, the photo’s own shingle and sky colors, one accent on the numeral. Grotesque, heavy, short. No script over shingles.

**Do.**

- Crop to the work: shingles, flashing, a person on the roof looking at the work.
- Put the offer in a flat panel on the quiet part of the sky or on a solid band, never in thin type across texture.
- If financing is real, it is the offer on its own card, or one subordinate line. It does not share the hero with a storm headline.
- Storm language only when the drop is actually weather-triggered and the claim is true.

**Do not.**

- Stock lightning, a stock family pointing at a roof, or a seal that looks like a city notice.
- A fear headline with no next step.
- Two trades at once (roof and paint, or roof and gutters) as equal columns.
- Humor with no offer. A joke can be the headline only if the offer and the phone are still obvious.

**Corpus note.** The 574–588 set is one vendor’s house style. Treat repeated bands, yellow tiles, and red/navy as that vendor’s grammar, not as independent proof. The transferable part is: free first step, real job photos, offer tile on a quiet field. Mailbox piece PM-044 shows the failure of two equal exterior offers. Prefer the single-numeral structure of PM-047 (a window card) as the roofing pattern: result photo, one number, one phone.

## Plumbing

**Buyer blocker.** Urgency plus distrust of the bill. The reader wants to know who is coming and what it costs.

**Lead with.** Either the problem and the tool (a part, a drain, hands at work) or the technician cropped mid-job, gaze on the work. One price. A phone that is large because the job is often a call, not a browse.

**Palette and type.** High contrast. One accent (red or a deep teal) reserved for the price or the phone, not both at full volume. Avoid a full-bleed yellow field unless the piece is honestly a coupon, and even then keep a single price.

**Do.**

- One number, one job (“drain clear,” “water heater check”).
- Show gloves, the tool, and the equipment. Skip the thumbs-up.
- License number as the single trust line, small, under the mark.

**Do not.**

- Three equal prices in a scalloped rail (the mailbox failure mode).
- A diagonal slogan across an empty address face.
- Cartoon water drops plus a stock family plus a coupon.

**Corpus note.** Stronger mailbox patterns: a person at the equipment with one price circle, and a single phone. Weaker: three-price rails and yellow template headers. There is no A plumbing card in this corpus. Do not average the C+ coupons into a target.

## HVAC

**Buyer blocker.** Seasonal discomfort and fear of a replacement sales pitch.

**Lead with.** The season in one line, plus one service (tune-up, replacement consult, or duct result). Photography of the equipment in the house or of the crew at the machine. A before/after of ducts only if the after is visibly cleaner. If both frames look the same, do not use the device.

**Palette and type.** Cool neutrals or the equipment’s own color. One warm accent if the season is heat, one cool accent if the season is cooling. Do not illustrate thermometers, snowflakes, and suns all on the same face.

**Do.**

- One season, one offer.
- The phone is primary when the promise is same-day. The QR can book a tune-up when the promise is scheduled.
- A branded van is allowed as proof of locality, with the phone repeated as type.

**Do not.**

- A door-hanger collage of six services and three offers.
- Stock family on a sofa under a vent.
- “24/7” icons as decoration when the offer is a two-week tune-up window.

**Corpus note.** The HVAC samples in the research images are mostly C. The mailbox heating card with a strip of real job photos and one red phone is the best of that set, and it is still a B− because the headline, the oval, and the phone share one volume. Push the phone or the offer to 35–50% and quiet the rest.

## Medicare / insurance (compliance-calm)

**Buyer blocker.** Confusion and fear of a scam. The reader is alert to anything that looks like the government or a fake deadline.

**Lead with.** A real local person (the advisor or a capable member), plain language, and a large calm field. The offer is help (a review, a meeting, a no-cost consult), not a scream. If a specific plan claim needs carrier approval, do not invent it.

**Palette and type.** Warm gray, navy, off-white. One small accent. Large type, wide leading, short words. Serif is allowed for a single headline if it stays heavy enough to print. No blackletter, no eagle, no folio that mimics a notice.

**Do.**

- Name the agency or the agent in the layout. A faceless “benefits center” is a trust failure.
- Put the required disclaimer where it can be read (≥ 7 pt, contrast ≥ 4.5:1), not as camouflage.
- Use one response: a phone or a mail-back, with a QR as the secondary inside the same lockup.
- Show active, capable people. A seminar or a meal can be the subject when the event is real.

**Do not.**

- Government seals, “official notice,” “final notice,” treasury-style engraving, or a red “respond within 5 days” on a lookalike.
- A tiny “not affiliated” line under a design that still looks federal.
- Stock seniors pointing at a laptop as the only proof.
- Fear of loss (“you will lose your benefits”) as the dominant line.

**Compliance is a design constraint, not a footnote.** Pieces that borrow the look of a government notice are anti-examples even when the hierarchy is loud. See analyses for MMR-513 and MMR-047. Serious branded design can still be clear and large. Looking official is not the same as looking governmental.

**Corpus note.** The calmer advisor and member pieces (a real face, a lot of paper, one invitation) are the pattern. National carrier self-mailers are style reference only: study the restraint, do not copy the marks. No piece in this corpus is an A Medicare postcard. Aim at the principles, not at an average of the samples.

## Real estate

**Buyer blocker.** The reader can look up listings. Mail has to feel like a specific house or a specific local fact, not a portal screenshot.

**Lead with.** One property or one street, photographed straight, with the one number that matters (price, or a single appointment offer). Huge type, small mark.

**Palette and type.** The photograph’s materials (stucco, wood, sky) plus near-black or white. No gold script. No faux-luxury serif on a gradient.

**Do.**

- Bleed the house. Put type in the sky or on a solid band.
- One fact. A second fact (open-house time, or a single next step) is the response, not a feature list.
- Agent portrait only as a small, real headshot tied to the phone, not as a second hero unless the piece is an introduction and says so.

**Do not.**

- Gold script, marble textures, pillar graphics, “just listed” starbursts, or a collage of six listings.
- A QR to a portal home page with no property.

**Corpus note.** This research set has **no real-estate postcard**. Guidance here comes from the anti-pattern notes (AP-009) and `references/category-visual-language.md`, not from a graded mailer. Do not invent a corpus grade for the vertical. Phase 2 should add real pieces before treating any real-estate template as calibrated.

## General local service

**Buyer blocker.** “Who is this, and why should I bother?” The reader does not have a trade-specific fear yet. The card has to pick one.

**Lead with.** One job the company actually wants this month, shown in one photo, with one number and one phone. The category word must be readable without the logo.

**Palette and type.** The company’s real pair, applied with the global rules: one accent, three sizes, one lockup. If the brand arrives as five colors and two scripts, the art director cuts to one accent and one family for the card, and keeps the script only inside the logo.

**Do.**

- Choose the blocker on purpose: price, speed, craft, or locality. Do not address all four.
- Repeat the response on the back.
- Match imagery to the blocker (P6): speed shows the crew in motion, craft shows the finished surface, locality shows a recognizable street or the real truck, price shows the numeral.

**Do not.**

- A category list as the headline (TP-12).
- Clip art next to photography.
- A different visual system on every drop.

**Corpus note.** The mailbox is dominated by competent C+ and B− local cards: yellow headers, equal photo strips, script logos, bare QR codes. The A pieces in that same mailbox are the ones that deleted the second idea. Use those, not the average, as the target. Weight the mailbox analyses over the vendor samples when they disagree, because the mailbox is what a resident actually received.
