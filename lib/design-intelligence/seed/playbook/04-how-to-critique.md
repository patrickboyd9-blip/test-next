# How to critique a card

Run this after the auto-fail checklist in the rubric (AF1–AF10). Auto-fails are not averaged away. A card with an auto-fail does not ship, whatever the taste score.

## Setup

1. Read the Design Rationale first if one exists (`schemas/design-rationale.schema.json`). Critique the card against its own stated focal element, path, and ratios. A pretty card that ignored its rationale still fails the system.
2. Judge the printed piece, not the table, the rotation of the snapshot, or the glare. If a committed photo crops the piece, say so and score only what is visible.
3. Do not transcribe headlines, phone numbers, codes, or addresses into notes. Paraphrase the offer type.

## The pass

Score each item 1–5. Anchors: **1** broken, **3** competent template, **5** the principle is doing the job with nothing extra.

| # | Principle | 5 means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Focal | One element, about 35–50% of the visual weight, still there when blurred |
| 2 | Hierarchy | Offer, headline, subhead, and body sit in the intended ratio; ≤ 3 sizes on the front |
| 3 | Balance | Weight is intentional; symmetry only where the vertical earns it |
| 4 | Grid | One named grid; alignments share an axis; at most one break |
| 5 | Eye path | Three stops end at the response |
| 6 | Gestalt | Offer is one group, response is one group, no orphan icons |
| 7 | Contrast | Primary type ≥ 4.5:1, house target ≥ 7:1, no outline type |
| 8 | White space | ≥ 25% calm field; front fact budget respected |
| 9 | Figure-ground | The figure separates in grayscale |
| 10 | Color | One neutral, one accent, emotion matches the blocker |
| 11 | Type | One family, readable measure and leading, no script headline |
| 12 | Photo | One subject, right crop, gaze and light match the blocker |
| 13 | Offer | One offer; numeral is hero when a number exists; qualifier attached |
| 14 | CTA | One primary device, labeled QR if present, phone at the print floor |
| 15 | Trust | One subordinate line; no badge wall; no government cues |
| 16 | Glance | Three seconds recall category, offer, and next step |

Overall grade, after auto-fails:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | No auto-fail. Glance passes. Focal, hierarchy, offer, and response are all 5 or a single 4. Nothing extra. |
| B | No auto-fail. The idea is clear and one or two principles are soft (a second small offer, a bare QR, a slightly even grid). |
| C | Competent category template. Readable, and several principles tie. This is the band our generator sits in today. |
| D | The reader cannot find one idea, or the photo/type relationship fails, even if some pieces are loud. |
| F | Auto-fail on contrast, overflow, missing response, or a government-notice / fake-deadline design. Loud hierarchy does not save an F. |

## What to emulate vs what not to copy

Write both lists every time.

- **Emulate** is a transferable move: “one numeral on a quiet panel over a result photo,” “phone and QR in one lower band.”
- **Do not copy** is brand-owned: marks, headlines, phone numbers, partner logos, agent portraits, illustration systems, the exact palette of a national carrier.
- **Anti-patterns** map to AP-001–AP-012 when they match, plus the mail-specific bans (government look, fake deadline, second hero offer).

Brand-owned files are style reference only. They may teach a principle. They are not source art.

## Weighting the corpus

When two references disagree, prefer a photographed mailbox piece (PM-001–PM-054) over a vendor sample (MMR), because the mailbox piece is what actually arrived. Prefer an A over an average. Do not promote a repeated vendor template into a rule just because fifteen cards share it.

## Output of a critique

The critic returns the scores, the grade, the first broken rule, and one edit that would move the card up a grade. The edit is structural (“delete the second price and enlarge the first”), not cosmetic (“nudge the logo”).
